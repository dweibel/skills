# Quick Reference: Long-Form Essay

Architectural composure with pedagogical curiosity. Composure wins when they clash.

## Opening

- First paragraph names the problem and its cost.
- Promise a shape, not a conclusion.
- No throat-clearing, no dictionary definitions, no hype.

## Section Shape

- H2 sections, each addressing one discrete question.
- Section opens with what it will resolve, works through the mechanism, closes with the implication for practice.
- Headings are noun phrases or short declarative claims. Sentence-case.
- H3 sparingly. Never H4.

## Pedagogy

- Why before what. Name the friction before the pattern.
- Mechanism over metaphor. Walk through what actually happens.
- Examples carry the weight. Concrete artifacts every few paragraphs.
- Dialectical exposition when two approaches compete. Always end with a position.

## Paragraphs and Lists

- Vary paragraph length. Single-sentence paragraphs are effective.
- Lists should be asymmetric. Match item length to idea weight.

## Closing

- Distill the argument, name the next open question, or return to the opening.
- Never recap.

## Length

- Short essay: 500–1,500 words. One claim, one or two supporting passes.
- Structured guide: 2,500–6,000 words. Multi-section, named trade-offs, closing synthesis.
- If it's 3,000 words saying what 800 could say, it's too long.

## Voice Reminders

- Active voice. Concrete nouns over pronouns. Plain verbs.
- Vary sentence length sharply. Four-word sentences next to thirty-word sentences.
- No banned vocabulary. No em dashes. No feigning surprise.
