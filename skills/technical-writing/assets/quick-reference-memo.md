# Quick Reference: Technical Memo / RFC

Organizational clarity template. Composed, curious voice inside.

## Structure (in this order)

1. **TL;DR / Decision** — recommendation in the first sentence. One paragraph max.
2. **Context / Background** — why this memo exists now. Keep short.
3. **Problem** — specific friction, with numbers where possible.
4. **Options Considered** — each option gets: description, what it buys, what it costs, when it's the right call.
5. **Recommendation** — the chosen option and the assumptions it rests on.
6. **What We Are Not Doing** — pre-empts "but what about X" by naming X and scoping it out.
7. **Risks and Mitigations** — specific failure modes paired with detection/mitigation.
8. **Open Questions** — gaps the memo cannot resolve alone.
9. **Action Items** — specific, owned, dated. Not vague aspirations.

## Conventions

- Headings are decisions or questions, not descriptions.
- Bold sparingly: key terms, decisions, explicit yes/no.
- Numbers carry trust. Quote real data when you have it.
- Lists are asymmetric. Weight items by importance.
- Length follows stakes. Two pages is fine. Twenty pages is a doc, not a memo.

## Voice Reminders

- Active voice. Plain verbs. No banned vocabulary.
- No em dashes. No feigning surprise. No fence-sitting.
- Take a position. Name the threshold where it flips.
